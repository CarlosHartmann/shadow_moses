This step ("forest recon") turns the study comments into discussions: the conversations in which "OP" is referred to. At this stage only the structure is built, from comment, parent and submission ids (the index described in forest_recon_index.md); no text is read. The text is extracted from the dumps afterwards, in one pass, for exactly the comments and submissions the structure names.

Definitions
- A mention is a comment containing "OP" (case-sensitive, standalone), i.e. any comment of ../../02-filtering/filtered_comments.jsonl (53,006,559 comments), not only the study comments. "OP" in submissions is not a mention: there it refers to someone other than the poster.
- The seeds are the 3,150,973 comments of the finalised study datasets (as in ../../07-unique-usernames-and-subreddits), baseline included.
- For each seed, the initial mention is the topmost mention on its path from the post down to the seed (the seed itself if there is none above it). Every mention below it on that path belongs to the same discussion. Mentions in other branches start their own discussions, since their speakers need not have read the other branch.
- A discussion is named after its initial mention: t1_<initial mention id>. Discussions never overlap.
- A discussion consists of:
  - the submission (title, content, author, flairs);
  - the context: the comments from the one replying to the submission down to the parent of the initial mention (only that chain, without its side branches);
  - the body: the initial mention and everything below it, all branches;
  - at the end, as evidence of whether OP's gender was known, the comments of the submission's author anywhere in the thread.
- Only discussions containing at least one seed are built.
- Comments by the submission's author that contain "OP" (e.g. "OP here") stay in the discussion but are not mentions. Authors are not in the index, so this is sorted out after the text extraction (or by the LLM step).
- Anaphora are later counted only for users whose metadata are confirmed (the study groups); the other comments are context.

Scripts (run in this order; all write to work/, which is gitignored)
1. prepare_ids.py: op_ids.parquet (all mention ids) and seeds.parquet (seed ids with thread, time, subreddit and study groups). About 1 min; reads only the internal disk.
2. scan_threads.py: one scan of the index's by_id copy (200 files, 108.7 GB on rdisk2), keeping every comment of the seeds' threads (~2.5 million submissions). One output file per index file; resumable; logs rate and ETA after each file.
3. build_forest.py: builds the discussions from those rows, without reading rdisk2. It first splits the rows into buckets of about 1.5 million (work/forest/rows_by_bucket/), then builds bucket by bucket. Outputs in work/forest/, one part-NNNN.parquet per bucket, so an interrupted build resumes: discussions/ (one row per discussion with size, depth and branching), members/ (every comment of every discussion with its role, depth, parent and flags), seed_map/ (every seed with its discussion and whether it is the initial mention). About 1.2 GB of memory.
4. export_extraction_ids.py: the id lists for the text extraction (work/extraction_ids/: comment ids per UTC month, i.e. per RC dump file; submission ids; manifest.tsv with the counts). Copied to otacon/past_commands/forest_text_extraction/ids/, where ORDER.md specifies the extraction for an otacon agent.
5. (otacon) The extraction of the texts from the dumps, as specified in otacon/past_commands/forest_text_extraction/ORDER.md; see REPORT.md there. Output in otacon_output/ (gitignored, 45 GB of verbatim NDJSON lines). compress_otacon_output.zsh then compresses these files in place to .ndjson.zst (zstd level 19; each archive is tested before the original is removed), which texts_to_parquet.py reads as well.
6. texts_to_parquet.py: those lines as Parquet with one schema per kind (work/texts/RC, work/texts/RS; 5.1 GB). About 1.5 min with 6 workers.
7. render_discussions.py: every discussion as one readable text (work/discussions/part-NNNN.parquet, one row per discussion with its study comments and their numbers in the text). `render_discussions.py --show t1_xxxxxxx` prints one.
8. detect_bots.py: bot accounts and template comments among all extracted comments (work/bots/); render_discussions.py then excludes them as study comments. About 1.5 min.
Comment and submission ids are stored as integers (base 36 decoded, as in the index); discussion ids as "t1_" + base 36.

Pilot (2026-10-27)
- Scan of 9 index files (part-029-031, 109-111, 179-181; 4.7 GB) took 9.5 min, at about 8 MB/s while the sayer-histories extraction was reading the same disk. 30.7 million rows were kept (186 MB).
- Forest for the 46,262 seeds of the three middle files (1.5% of all seeds), in 49 s:
  - 87% of the seeds are initial mentions; 13% belong to a discussion started higher up. 44,017 discussions.
  - Body size (initial mention and everything below): median 2 comments, 90% up to 8, 99% up to 42, 99.9% up to 247, maximum 2,787. 49% of the bodies are the initial mention alone.
  - Body depth: median 1, 99% up to 13, maximum 989 (a chain of single replies). Most replies to one comment: median 1, 99% up to 8, maximum 1,183.
  - Context size: median 1, 99% up to 9, maximum 803.
  - 0.25% of the upward walks stop below the top (ancestor not in the index; partly an effect of the pilot reading only three files per period).
  - About 6 comments per discussion in total.
- Extrapolated to all seeds: about 3 million discussions with about 18 million comments, plus ~2.5 million submissions and the submitters' comments. The full scan should take about 4 h (less with an idle disk), the forest build well under 1 h.

Full run (2026-10-27)
- Scan: 191 index files (104 GB; the 9 pilot files were reused) in 1 h 21 min, at about 21 MB/s while the sayer-histories extraction was reading the same disk. 745.5 million thread rows in total (4.4 GB).
- Build: a first version re-read all rows for each of its 150 buckets (7.5 min per bucket, 52 GB of DuckDB spill files); a second one held 6 GB of memory and stalled in swap at bucket 141, next to the sayer workers (about 10 GB on a 16 GB machine). Neither left usable output. The present version (smaller buckets, one file per bucket, about 1.2 GB) ran overnight next to the sayer extraction: split 50 min, 498 buckets in 4 h 33 min (done 05:35 on 2026-10-28). The Python process then did not exit (probably swapped out while shutting down) and was stopped after 20 min by a watchdog; all output files had been written and renamed by then.

Results of the full build
- All 3,150,973 seeds have a discussion: 2,765,376 (88%) are initial mentions, 385,597 belong to a discussion started higher up. None is missing from the index (this includes the 9 comments that were first handled by hand, see below).
- 3,006,374 discussions in 2,514,433 threads.
- Body size: median 2, 90% up to 7, 99% up to 42, 99.9% up to 227, maximum 38,261; 50% of the bodies are the initial mention alone. Body depth: median 1, 99% up to 13, maximum 29,364. Most replies to one comment: 99% up to 8, maximum 2,180. Context: median 1, 99% up to 10, maximum 2,819. Seeds per discussion: 99% up to 2, maximum 60.
- 223 upward walks (0.007%) stop below the top.
- 18,041,469 member rows; 17,861,694 distinct comments (13,020,422 in bodies, 4,841,272 in contexts; contexts are shared by sibling discussions).
- Heavy discussions (body above 250 comments or deeper than 50 levels): 3,307, with 1.28 million body comments (10% of all body comments) but only 7,811 seeds (0.25%). The largest, t1_c3ntogw, is a chain of 38,261 comments, 29,364 levels deep.
- To extract: 17,861,694 comments and 2,514,433 submissions, plus the submitters' comments in those threads (not countable yet: the index has no authors).

Limitations
- Replies after 2021-09 are not in the index, so discussions from the last weeks of September 2021 can lack later replies.
- Mentions in comments that were deleted or removed before being archived cannot be seen; a lower mention then counts as the initial one.
- Lowercase "op" and bot comments (filtered out in step 2) are not mentions.
- A study user's comment without "OP" (anaphora only) is included only if it lies in a discussion that some seed starts or belongs to.
- Ancestors from before 2010 and comments posted during the gaps of the dumps (forest_recon_index.md, Q5; mostly 2010-2012) are missing; such upward walks stop early and are flagged (upward_complete = false).

Text extraction and rendering (2026-10-29)
- The otacon extraction found all 17,861,694 listed comments and 2,511,879 of the 2,514,433 submissions; 2,554 submissions are in no RS file. It also took 16,118,574 comments by the submissions' authors in those threads. 391,663 of the found submissions (15.6%) have a deleted author, so their authors' other comments cannot be identified.
- Discussions whose submission is missing are discarded (decision of 2026-10-29): 2,771 discussions with 2,864 study comments (0.09%; at most 0.5% of any group, gender_political_conservative 320 of 65,462). They are listed in work/discussions/discarded.tsv.
- The post author's other comments (decision of 2026-10-29): only those posted before the discussion's last study comment (what its speakers could have seen), the earliest 50 at most, with a note on how many were left out. All of them would have been 31.5 million entries, more than the discussions themselves; some threads have thousands (at most 140,171).
- Heavy discussions (body above 250 comments or deeper than 50 levels; 3,307 discussions, 7,811 study comments). The guess was that they are counting games. Samples of each kind were read (2026-10-29), and they are not: only 32 are in r/counting. 1,870 are wide (a comment with hundreds of replies, e.g. top verdicts in AmItheAsshole, comments on pics and funny posts), 462 are long chains (mostly two people arguing for 100-200 turns, e.g. in relationship_advice, Christianity, atheism), 660 are mixed and 315 have a long context. Their study comments use OP anaphora like any other ("If OP really thinks ... he wouldn't capitulate", "the OP inferred s/he was aware of ..."). So they are kept, but pruned in the rendering: the initial mention; for each study comment up to 20 comments above it and its first 20 replies; the top of the context chain and its last 20 comments; a note gives the number left out. The full structure stays in work/forest/, so the cap can be changed later.
- Joke-like heavy discussions are dropped (decision of 2026-10-29): of the heavy discussions, the 242 where more than 30% of the body comments repeat another one (compared in lower case, letters, digits and spaces only) or more than half are shorter than 25 characters ("invest 100" in MemeEconomy, "spin", "F", RemindMe, counting, role-play chains). They hold 390 study comments. Listed in work/discussions/discarded.tsv with the reason.
- Rendered: 3,003,361 discussions with 3,147,719 study comments (3,064 of them pruned); discarded: 2,771 without a submission (2,864 study comments) and 242 joke-like (390).
- HTML entities (&amp;, &gt;, &lt;) are turned back into plain characters in the rendered texts.
- The 9 comments from six subreddits that were first thought not to be indexed (ClassicMoviePosters, NichirenBuddhism, SeattleHistory, fightingillini, letters, seattlehobos) are in the index after all, so they are rendered here like all others. Their hand-made contexts (../manual/) were removed on 2026-11-16.

Bot filter (2026-10-29)
- Step 2 removed authors whose names end in "Bot", "Mod" or "Moderator", case-sensitive (as its instructions say). That let bots such as wikipedia_text_bot, remindditbot and kzreminderbot through, and a case-insensitive name rule would also remove people (Accountantnotbot, Chimpbot, Tombot3000 write ordinary comments) while still missing bots (samacharbot2, Judgement_Bot_AITA, Dad_B0T). So the filter is applied here, in step 8, and judges accounts by what they write, over all their extracted comments (34 million): see detect_bots.py for the rules.
- Decision (2026-10-29): apply it in step 8, not in step 2 with a re-run. For human users both give the same study comments in the same groups; the difference is the baseline, which keeps 975,109 of its 1,000,000 comments instead of being re-sampled, and discussion boundaries where a bot's "OP" comment is the initial mention (it still names the discussion; its text stays as context).
- Checked by reading samples: 28 of 30 bot accounts are bots (the other two, dcorbe and Deawesomerx, are people posting scripted messages); 25 of 25 template study comments are templates (mostly moderators' removal notices, e.g. "This comment or post has been removed for derailing ... Changing the topic from OP's question"). Known bots are all caught, by the account rules or (BernardJOrtcutt, iamveryrobotic, VannaMayo) by the template rule. A rule on repeated six-word openings for single comments was tried and dropped: it also hits people's habitual openers ("Hey, OP! I hope you are doing well!").
- Result: 439 bot accounts (226 disclosing, 197 bot name and repetitive, 16 repeated openings) with 120,875 study comments, and 45,879 template study comments by other authors: 166,754 of 3,150,973 (5.3%). By group: age_Z -15.4% and age_young -10.2% (wikipedia_text_bot 53,076, remindditbot 38,076), political_conservative -7.2% (unRealityEngineer's "Hey OP, reply to each comment to reap sweet comment Karma"), gender_female -5.5% (moderator templates in AskWomen: kaeorin, nevertruly, MostlyALurkerBefore), gender_male -2.6% (kzreminderbot), baseline -2.5%, political_progressive -1.6%, all others at most 0.4%.
- How the bots got into the groups: through flairs that the annotation in ../../03a-grouping/grouped_flairs.xlsx rightly reads as group evidence. wikipedia_text_bot and remindditbot each have 2 comments in r/GenZ with the flair "2008" (given_birthyear 2008, hence age_Z); kzreminderbot has 10 in r/askteenboys with "M" (gender_male, relevant); unRealityEngineer 1 in r/Conservative with "Reagan Conservative" (relevant). Bot accounts can carry flairs, and step 4 then added all their other comments (53,076 for wikipedia_text_bot).
- Rendered: 2,837,950 discussions with 2,981,193 study comments. Discarded: 2,771 without a submission, 242 joke-like, 165,411 left without study comments. Bot accounts are marked "(bot)" in the texts (28,481 texts); excluded study comments are listed in work/discussions/excluded_study_comments.tsv. Every one of the 3,150,973 study comments is either rendered, excluded or in a discarded discussion.

Caps (2026-11-16)
The first caps (2026-10-29, described above: pruning only above 250 comments or 50 levels, up to 50 comments by the post's author, no limit on text length) left 427 texts above 100,000 characters and 23,237 above 30,000. Most Reddit discussions are short (median 3 comments in context and body, 99% up to 44), so stricter caps were decided, to keep weird, freakish and meme-like material out without losing ordinary discussions. They replace the earlier figures:
- Long discussions, with more than 50 comments in context and body, are pruned: the initial mention; for each study comment up to 10 comments above it and its first 10 replies; the top of the context chain and its last 10 comments. 24,266 discussions (0.9%) with 40,036 study comments are pruned; none of their study comments is lost.
- The post author's other comments: the earliest 20 (before the last study comment), each cut at 1,500 characters. 129,986 discussions have more than 20.
- Long texts are cut with a note of how many characters follow: the post's content at 10,000 characters, comments at 5,000. Study comments are never cut. 68,141 texts contain a cut. Comments by other users than the study users are cut too, so an anaphor after the 5,000th character of such a comment is not seen.
- Joke-like long discussions are dropped as before (more than 30% repeated comments or more than half shorter than 25 characters), now among all discussions with more than 50 comments: 481 discussions with 724 study comments (AskReddit, teenagers, Tinder, meme subreddits). Deleted and removed comments are not counted as repeats any more: otherwise heavily moderated threads (relationships, personalfinance, legaladvice) count as jokes.
- Texts still longer than 40,000 characters are dropped: 1,940 discussions with 3,655 study comments (0.12%). These are mostly long debates (changemyview 234, PurplePillDebate 103, DebateReligion 102), not jokes: the price of a strict limit.
- Result: 2,835,870 discussions with 2,977,352 study comments; median text 2,384 characters, 99% up to 19,505, longest 39,991. Discarded: 2,771 without a submission, 165,312 with only bot or template study comments, 481 joke-like, 1,940 too long. All 3,150,973 study comments are rendered, excluded (166,754) or in a discarded discussion.
