This step looks at the people in the discussions (step 8) beyond the study users, and fixes the final group of every user. The study users were found through comments that contain "OP" and carry a relevant flair (steps 2-4). The discussions also hold comments by other people, who may refer to OP without writing "OP", and comments without "OP" by baseline users. Flairs on those comments are evidence that steps 3-4 never saw. If such a flair places a user in one of the study groups, their language is data too, and a baseline user with such a flair does not belong in the baseline. Bots are left out: their accounts are known from step 8 (../08-full-contexts/programmatic/work/bots/bot_accounts.tsv).

Decisions
- 2026-10-30: Not yet annotated subreddit and flair combinations go into a separate workbook, new_flairs.xlsx, laid out like ../03a-grouping/grouped_flairs.xlsx. grouped_flairs.xlsx stays untouched.
- 2026-10-30: Once an additional user qualifies for a group, all their comments in the discussions count for it (as in step 4), in any subreddit.
- 2026-11-16: Baseline users whose flairs admit them to a group are moved out of the baseline (baseline_users_moved.tsv). A flair that the annotation counts as group evidence rules its user out of the baseline, wherever the flair was seen.
- 2026-11-16: new_flairs.xlsx is annotated only in part, as far as time allows, from the top of each sheet down (see "Partial annotation"). The script reads both workbooks and can be rerun at any time.
- 2026-11-16: Conflicts are not decided by hand here (step 5 did that for the study users). A user admitted to both groups of a family is put in neither; a user whose age flairs have no common birth year gets no age group (conflicts.tsv). A single user can still be decided through the user_group_overrides and age_user_overrides sheets of grouped_flairs.xlsx, which the script obeys.

Script
discussion_users.py (about 1.5 min; run it from anywhere with the repo's poetry environment). For every author of a comment shown in the rendered discussions (../08-full-contexts/programmatic/work/discussions/, comment_ids), all their extracted comments in the group subreddits are checked with the rules of step 3a (subreddit lists, score filter, relevant flairs, pre-2012 high-likelihood rule, user_group_overrides; ../03a-grouping/grouping.py) and, for age, the birth-year deduction of step 3b (../03b-age-grouping/deduce_birthyear.py). Evidence comes only from comments in the discussions' threads (all extracted ones, also those not shown in the texts), not from the users' whole histories.
It writes:
- new_flairs.xlsx: the combinations that grouped_flairs.xlsx does not have, with their annotations (see below).
- data/user_groups.parquet (gitignored): the final table for the later steps. One row per user: every study user (bots excluded) and every additional user admitted to a group, with their groups, how they were found (route: op_comment = steps 3-5; baseline; baseline_moved; discussion), the birth-year range for users given an age group here, and the number of their comments shown in the discussions (and how many of those contain "OP"). Later steps should take users' groups from this table, not from the group files of steps 3-6.
- data/admitted_comments.parquet (gitignored): the flaired comments that admit users, and whether a new_flairs.xlsx annotation was needed for it.
- baseline_users_moved.tsv, conflicts.tsv, summary.txt (counts per group and route, comments per year, state of the annotation).

Age groups are given per user here: the birth-year ranges of all the user's age flairs are intersected (highest first year, lowest last year), and the generation and young/old follow from that range as in step 3b (majority of the years; older group on a tie).

new_flairs.xlsx
- One sheet per group, as in grouped_flairs.xlsx; a combination is on the sheet of every group whose subreddit list contains its subreddit. The first columns are those of grouped_flairs.xlsx and are annotated in the same way ("relevant": any mark; age sheet: given_age etc.).
- checked: fill in anything (e.g. "n") for a combination that was looked at and is not relevant. A row counts as annotated if it has an annotation or a checked mark; empty rows count as not yet looked at, and their users stay out of the group.
- gain, users, comments, first_year, example: refreshed on every run. users: the users with this flair in one of the group's subreddits who are not study users of any group; comments: their comments shown in the discussions; first_year: the earliest of those; example: a link to a comment with the flair.
- gain: by how many percent the combination's users would raise the number of the group's users in the discussions, summed over the years 2010-2021. For each year, the combination's users with comments in the discussions are divided by the group's study users with comments in the discussions in that year. A user in a sparse early year therefore counts more than one in 2020. The yardstick only uses the study users of steps 3-5, so a combination's gain does not change as the annotation proceeds. The sheets are sorted by gain.
- Rerunning keeps all annotation and checked cells (also of combinations that no longer occur) and saves a copy of the previous workbook in annotation_backup/ (gitignored). Close the workbook in Excel before running the script. Commit new_flairs.xlsx after annotating: the outputs follow from the two workbooks and the step 8 data alone.

Partial annotation
- Nearly every new combination (about 99%) is carried by a single user, since the frequent flairs were already annotated in step 3. Annotating a row can thus add one user at most, and many rows are not relevant for the sheet they are on (a gender flair on a political sheet). The whole workbook (8,500 combinations, 25,000 rows) could add a few thousand users at best, next to 39,912 additional users already admitted through the existing annotations.
- The rule that keeps a partial annotation describable: annotate each sheet from the top down, without skipping rows. Then the annotated part is "all combinations with a gain of at least g" for a g that summary.txt reports per sheet ("annotated without a gap down to gain"). The order depends only on when and how many users carried a flair, not on what they wrote, so the cut-off cannot favour a way of referring to OP.
- What the cut-off does change: the composition over time (early years are filled first, on purpose) and the share of users found through this route. Analyses should therefore keep the year and the route (user_groups: route) as variables, or check that results hold without the users of this route.
- Annotating more rows adds users. Two things can also take data away, both intended: a user who becomes admitted to both groups of a family drops out of both, and a baseline user who becomes admitted leaves the baseline.

Users of this route differ from the study users: they were not found through an "OP" comment. Of the comments of the study users of the groups shown in the discussions, about half contain "OP"; of the 267,458 comments of the users found here, 1.3% do; of those of the users moved from the baseline, 14% (they said "OP" in a thread, but never with a relevant flair in a group subreddit, or their "OP" comments were not sampled). Their anaphora for OP count all the same, but there will be fewer per comment.

Results (2026-11-16, after the stricter caps of step 8; no row of new_flairs.xlsx annotated yet), see summary.txt

Not done here
- Study users of the groups keep the groups of steps 3-5; flairs seen in the discussions are not used to add further groups to them (e.g. an age group for a gender_male user). Steps 3-5 already give a user every group for which a relevant flair sits on one of their "OP" comments; what is left out is flairs that these users only carried on comments without "OP". A check on 2026-11-16 (not scripted in the repo): 1,375 of the 70,125 study users of the groups would gain a further group that way (age 334, gender_male 352, gender_female 194, political_conservative 35, political_progressive 53, gender_political_conservative 17, gender_political_progressive 412), and 114 would get a flair of the group opposite to their own (110 of them male/female).
